## The weather you are packing for

Monthly mean air temperatures at Peninsula research stations between November and March sit between about −2 °C and +2 °C (see the [season chart](/season)). Wind and spray make it feel colder on deck and in small boats.

## Clean gear: the biosecurity rules

The Antarctic Treaty prohibits introducing non-native species. IAATO's "Don't Pack a Pest!" guidance asks visitors to:

- inspect and clean boots, trouser cuffs, pockets, fleece, backpacks, poles and tripods before leaving home;
- avoid clothing and equipment with Velcro, which traps seeds;
- use the ship's boot-wash station before every landing and on returning to the ship.

South Georgia's rules go further: every visitor must watch the government's briefing film before arrival, daypacks and camera bags are brushed and vacuumed, boot washing is mandatory before and after every landing, and visitors keep at least 5 metres from wildlife.

## What operators often provide

Check with your operator. As examples, Quark Expeditions says it lends insulated muck boots and gives each passenger a parka to keep, and Oceanwide Expeditions lists the use of waterproof muck boots and snowshoes as included. Not every operator does the same.

## Documents and medicine

UK government travel advice notes very limited medical facilities in the region and recommends travel insurance that specifies Antarctica. See [Do I need travel insurance with evacuation cover?](/questions/travel-insurance-evacuation-cover)
