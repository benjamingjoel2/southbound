## The landing rules

IAATO's bylaws set four operating rules for members whose ships make landings:

1. No more than **100 visitors ashore** at any one site at the same time.
2. Ships carrying **more than 500 passengers** may not make landings.
3. At least **one guiding staff member for every 20 visitors** ashore.
4. **Only one ship** at a landing site at a time.

The same four limits appear in the Antarctic Treaty's Measure 15 (2009). The Treaty Secretariat lists that measure as "not yet effective", so the binding version today is IAATO's self-regulation for its members.

## What this means for passengers

- **Over 500 passengers:** no landings. These ships offer cruising only. IAATO's 2025-26 figures counted 27,217 cruise-only visitors.
- **Over 100 passengers:** the ship lands people in shifts so that no more than 100 are ashore at once. The more passengers, the more shifts, and the less time each person can spend ashore at a site.
- **100 passengers or fewer:** everyone can be ashore at the same time.

The rules do not say how long a shift lasts; that depends on the operator, the site and the weather.

## Small ships

Southbound does not define "small ship" with a number of its own. The landing limits above are the useful thresholds. For context, the South Georgia government notes that most of its visitors come on expedition ships carrying fewer than 400 passengers.
