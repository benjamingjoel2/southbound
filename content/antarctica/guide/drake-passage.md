The Drake Passage is an ocean gateway about 850 km wide between South America and the Antarctic Peninsula, according to the British Antarctic Survey.

## Why it can be rough

- It is the narrowest point (about 800 km) on the path of the Antarctic Circumpolar Current, which is driven by westerly winds.
- It lies in the belt of strong westerlies. NOAA describes the "Roaring Forties" and, ten degrees further south, the even stronger "Furious Fifties".
- The British Antarctic Survey explains that these westerlies are especially strong because there are no continents in the Southern Ocean to slow them down.

How rough a given crossing is depends on the weather systems passing at the time. See [Is the Drake Passage really that rough?](/questions/is-the-drake-passage-rough)

## How long the crossing takes

We have not found a non-commercial source for crossing time. One operator's published 10-night itinerary from Ushuaia spends two days crossing each way. The straight-line distance from Ushuaia to Deception Island in the South Shetlands is about 1,008 km (544 nautical miles), by our calculation; the sailed route is longer.
