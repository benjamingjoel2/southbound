Antarctica is a continent, not a country. It is governed under the Antarctic Treaty. For passengers on an organised visit, UK government travel advice notes that the tour operator usually arranges the permit needed. Tourist trips cluster in a few regions.

## Antarctic Peninsula

The long arm of the continent that reaches north towards South America. IAATO, the tour operators' association, reports that 98% of tourism voyages operate in the Antarctic Peninsula region. This is where a "classic" Antarctic voyage goes.

## South Shetland Islands

A chain of islands just north of the Peninsula, across the Drake Passage from South America. King George Island has the airstrip used by fly-cruise trips. Deception Island is also in the group.

## Weddell Sea

The sea on the eastern side of the Peninsula. Some longer voyages sail into it if ice conditions permit. Near its northern edge is Snow Hill Island, whose emperor penguin colony the British Antarctic Survey describes as the most northerly emperor breeding site. A survey team reached it in November 2023 using helicopters from a cruise ship.

## Ross Sea

On the opposite side of the continent, south of New Zealand. IAATO notes that a few cruises depart from Australia or New Zealand; Ross Sea voyages are long and infrequent. The Ross Sea region Marine Protected Area, covering 1.55 million km², was established on 1 December 2017.

## The sub-Antarctic: South Georgia and the Falklands

Longer voyages add islands north of the Antarctic Treaty area:

- **South Georgia** is a British Overseas Territory about 800 miles south-east of the Falklands. St Andrews Bay holds its largest king penguin colony. Visitors need an entry permit; see [Do I need a permit for South Georgia?](/questions/south-georgia-permit).
- **The Falkland Islands** are a British Overseas Territory. UK travel advice says visitors need a passport valid for the whole stay.
