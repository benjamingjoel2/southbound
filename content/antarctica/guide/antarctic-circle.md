## Where it is

The Antarctic Circle is the line of latitude south of which the sun stays above the horizon for a full day at least once a year. NOAA puts it at roughly 66° south. Its exact position is 90° minus the tilt of Earth's axis; using NOAA's equations, that is about 66°34′ S for 2027. It drifts very slightly from year to year.

On the Peninsula, the Circle lies south of the most visited sites such as Port Lockroy and the Lemaire Channel, so crossing it means sailing further south than a typical itinerary.

## When voyages cross it

Sea ice is at its yearly minimum in the southern summer (on 20 February in 2024, according to NASA), so ships have the best chance of getting that far south later in the season. A booking agent's listing describes circle-crossing voyages as typically 12 to 14 days, with the actual route depending on ice and weather.

## Daylight at the Circle

By our calculation with the NOAA equations, on 15 December 2026 the sun does not set at the Antarctic Circle; on 15 March 2027 it is up for about 12 hours 56 minutes.
