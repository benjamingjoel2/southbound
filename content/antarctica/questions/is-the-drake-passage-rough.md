The reputation has a physical basis:

- **The wind.** The passage sits in the belt of westerlies. NOAA describes the "Roaring Forties" and the even stronger gale-force "Furious Fifties" ten degrees further south. The British Antarctic Survey notes that these winds are especially strong because no continents in the Southern Ocean slow them down.
- **The current.** Those westerlies drive the Antarctic Circumpolar Current, and the Drake Passage is the narrowest point on its path, about 800 km across.

What no source can tell you is what *your* crossing will be like. That depends on the weather systems passing when you sail.

**How long it lasts.** One operator's published itinerary from Ushuaia spends two days crossing each way. Other itineraries differ.

**Avoiding it.** Fly-cruise trips fly from Punta Arenas, Chile, to King George Island and board the ship there. IAATO notes that these flights depend on the weather, so delays can happen.
