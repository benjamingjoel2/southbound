The British Antarctic Survey gives these dates for the two most closely timed Peninsula species:

| | November | January |
|---|---|---|
| **Adélie** | Eggs laid mid-November | Chicks hatched (late December), joining crèches at about three weeks |
| **Chinstrap** | Eggs laid late November | Chicks hatching (early January) |

**November** is the season of nests and eggs. It is also the month with the most ice; IAATO reported early-season ice blocking landing sites in 2024-25, so plans can change.

**January** is the season of chicks. Adélie chicks are in crèches and chinstrap chicks are hatching. By mid-February, Adélie chicks are at sea.

**Gentoo penguins** do not follow a tight calendar: the British Antarctic Survey says their breeding is not well synchronised, so you may see different stages at different colonies in either month.

**Emperor penguins** are a separate case: see [Can you see emperor penguins on a cruise?](/questions/can-you-see-emperor-penguins-on-a-cruise).
